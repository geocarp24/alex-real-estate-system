Hiring the wrong contractor is one of the most expensive mistakes a Wisconsin homeowner can make. We've seen it all — homeowners paying upfront for jobs that never started, projects abandoned mid-way, shoddy workmanship that costs twice as much to fix. Here are the 8 red flags that should make you walk away, no matter how cheap the quote sounds.

Red Flag #1: They Won't Show You a License

In Wisconsin, contractors handling jobs over $1,000 must be licensed. Ask to see the state-issued license number and verify it on the Wisconsin DSPS website. If they refuse, walk away.

Red Flag #2: No Proof of Insurance

Every legitimate contractor carries general liability and workers' compensation insurance. Ask for a certificate of insurance from their broker. If they get hurt on your property without coverage, you could be liable.

Red Flag #3: They Demand Large Upfront Payments

Wisconsin law allows contractors to ask for a reasonable deposit (typically 10–30%), but anyone demanding 50%+ upfront is either undercapitalized or planning to disappear with your money.

Red Flag #4: No Written Contract

Never start a project on a handshake. Every job — even small ones — should have a written contract listing scope, timeline, materials, payment schedule, and warranty terms.

Red Flag #5: Unusually Low Quotes

If three quotes come in around $40,000 and one comes in at $20,000, the cheap one isn't a deal — it's a warning. Either they're missing scope, planning change orders, or cutting corners.

Red Flag #6: No Local References

Ask for 3–5 references from completed Wisconsin projects in the last year. A real contractor will have plenty. Call them. Visit the work if you can.

Red Flag #7: They Pressure You to Decide Now

Honest contractors give you time to think. Sales pressure tactics ("this price is only good today") are designed to skip the comparison phase.

Red Flag #8: They Don't Pull Permits

If a contractor says "we don't need permits for this" — verify with your local Wisconsin building department. Contractors who skip permits skip inspections, and you're the one stuck with code violations when you sell.
